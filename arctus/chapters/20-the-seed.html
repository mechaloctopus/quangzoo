Analect Twenty

The Seed

"Make the most of the Indian hemp seed, and sow it everywhere."

There exists a single plant that can produce food, fiber, fuel, medicine, paper, plastic, and building material — all while growing faster than trees, needing less water than cotton, requiring no pesticides, and enriching the soil it grows in. It's been cultivated for ten thousand years. The first drafts of the Declaration of Independence were written on it. The word "canvas" comes from "cannabis." George Washington grew it. Thomas Jefferson grew it. And for eighty years the most versatile crop in agricultural history was illegal to plant.

Make the most of the Indian hemp seed, and sow it everywhere.

— George Washington, diary entry, 1794

What Hemp Can Do

FoodComplete protein (all 9 essential amino acids), optimal omega-3:omega-6 ratio, high in fiber, magnesium, iron, zinc (Callaway, 2004).
FiberStronger than cotton, more durable, more absorbent, no pesticides needed. One acre = 2-3x more fiber than one acre of cotton.

PlasticHemp cellulose = biodegradable bioplastic. Henry Ford built a hemp-composite car body in 1941.

MedicineCBD: FDA-approved for epilepsy (Epidiolex, 2018). Ongoing research in pain, anxiety, inflammation.

The Suppression

Hemp was legal and widely cultivated throughout American history until the Marihuana Tax Act of 1937 effectively criminalized all cannabis — including industrial hemp with negligible psychoactive content. The history involves industrial interests, racial politics, and media manipulation. What matters: a plant that could have provided sustainable alternatives to petroleum plastics, wood-pulp paper, and cotton textiles was removed from the landscape precisely when the petrochemical industry was developing synthetic alternatives. The 2018 Farm Bill re-legalized industrial hemp (less than 0.3% THC), but decades of prohibition destroyed agricultural knowledge, processing infrastructure, and market development.

Hemp and Sovereignty

The deepest argument for hemp is philosophical. It's the plant of sovereignty — the single crop closest to providing a community with the material basis for self-sufficiency: food, fiber, building material, paper, fuel, medicine. Each of these currently requires dependence on global supply chains vulnerable to disruption. Hemp reduces those dependencies across multiple categories from a single seed. The right to grow food is among the most fundamental rights a human can claim. Hemp is food. It's also shelter, clothing, and medicine. Its full normalization — as unremarkable as growing tomatoes — is the destination.

Why use up the forests which were centuries in the making and the mines which required ages to lay down, if we can get the equivalent of forest and mineral products in the annual growth of the hemp fields?

— Henry Ford (attributed, c. 1941)

The sovereign plant asks nothing but sunlight, water, and soil. It gives back everything a household needs to begin the journey toward independence. That journey continues in the next analect.
