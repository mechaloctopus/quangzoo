The Formula

"The illiterate of the 21st century will not be those who cannot read and write, but those who cannot learn, unlearn, and relearn."

There is a formula. Not a secret, not a hack, not a four-hour shortcut to mastery — a formula. A repeatable process for wrapping your mind around any concept, any field, any subject or object under the sun. It works for quantum physics and it works for sourdough. It works for coding websites and it works for understanding your own grief. The formula doesn't care what you point it at. It cares only that you follow the steps honestly and don't skip the ones that hurt.

Most people never learn how to learn. School taught them what to learn — facts, dates, procedures — but never the meta-skill: how to approach any unfamiliar territory and systematically make it familiar. This analect is that meta-skill. Internalize it and you will never again feel helpless in front of something you don't understand. You'll have the map. The territory still requires walking, but you'll know which direction to face.

Seven Phases. Skip None.

  • 1
    Bird's-Eye View. Before you dive into anything, step back and compile what is known about it in summary form. Use AI, Wikipedia, a documentary, the table of contents of the best introductory book. You're not mastering details yet. You're understanding the shape of the territory. Goal: explain the field in three minutes to someone who knows nothing about it. If you can't, your overview isn't done.
  • 2
    The Breakdown. Take your goal and break it into every component, sub-component, and dependency, down to the finest grain. This is where it gets daunting. The full scope of what you don't know becomes visible. Most people quit here. Don't. The discomfort is data. It's showing you exactly where the gaps are.
  • 3
    Key Principles. Every field rests on a small number of foundational principles. The Pareto principle applies: 20% of the principles drive 80% of the results. In physics it's conservation laws. In cooking it's heat transfer and seasoning. In business it's value creation and distribution. Find the 20%. Internalize it. The rest is refinement.
  • 4
    Touch the Territory. Do the thing. Reading about swimming is not swimming. Watching coding tutorials is not coding. Build the website and ship it broken. Cook the dish and serve it to someone honest. Play the song in front of humans. The map is not the territory, and the territory can only be known by walking it. This is where the body learns.
  • 5
    Deliberate Practice. Not just repetition — targeted work on the weakest sub-skills, at the edge of current ability, with immediate feedback. Playing your favorite song 10,000 times won't make you a virtuoso. Practicing the transitions you can't execute cleanly will. If it feels easy, you're not at the edge.
  • 6
    Cross-Pollination. Once you have a working understanding of one field, deliberately enter an unrelated one and repeat. Read about ice hockey. Skim turbine design. Flip through Vogue. The more domains you absorb, the more you see the archetypes repeating across all of them. This is not dilettantism. It's pattern recognition at scale.
  • 7
    Teach It. Explain what you've learned to someone else. If you can make a complex subject clear to a beginner — without oversimplifying, without jargon — you genuinely understand it. Where you stumble is where your understanding is weakest. Those stumbles are gifts.

Creativity is just connecting things. When you ask creative people how they did something, they feel a little guilty because they didn't really do it, they just saw something.

— Steve Jobs, Wired interview (1996)

The Emotional Arc

Learning follows a predictable emotional curve. Knowing the curve in advance strips it of its power to stop you:

Excitement → Overwhelm → The Dip (long slog, invisible progress, most people quit) → Breakthrough (suddenly things click) → Integration (the new knowledge weaves into everything else you know).

The arc repeats with every new domain. The more times you complete it, the faster it gets — because you carry the meta-skill forward and you start recognizing the archetypes across fields.

AI as Accelerant

For the first time in history, every person with internet access has a tireless research assistant available 24/7. This changes phases 1 and 3 from hours to minutes. Want to understand quantum entanglement? Ask for an overview, the ten key principles, the five best books, the three most common misconceptions. Fifteen minutes and the map is in your hands.

But — and this is critical — AI cannot walk the territory for you. It gives you the map. Your hands build the thing. Your body does the practice. Your voice teaches it. The understanding that lives in the bones can only be earned by the bones.

Use the machine to see farther. Use your hands to go deeper.

"Tell me and I forget, teach me and I may remember, involve me and I learn."

— Often attributed to Benjamin Franklin
